Attention Restoration Theory (ART) posits that nature and restorative breaks can renew cognitive focus. 
Voluntary directed attention requires active mental effort and is susceptible to mental fatigue over prolonged computer use.
Involuntary attention, on the other hand, is effortless. Incorporating micro-breaks of 3-5 minutes every 45 minutes of continuous cognitive strain allows directed attention fatigue to recover significantly.
When employees and developers ignore recovery periods, error rates in code compilation and document processing increase by up to 38% in the second half of the workday.
